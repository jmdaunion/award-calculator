Step 1 · Grade and salary scale

Your Emergency Duty hourly base

The Award prices duty off the midpoint of your grade's scale, not your own point: (Point 4 + Point 5) ÷ 2 ÷ 2,080 hours for Medical Officers 1 to 3. Interns, Locum Interns and Senior House Officers use their annual salary ÷ 2,080. (Award section 2(b) and 2(c); paragraph 78.) Your own point still matters for your basic salary and the net-pay step.

Medical Officers 1 start at point 2: point 1 of the MO1 scale is the Senior House Officer salary, and the Ministry of Health's letter to the Regions of 7 November 2024 directs that any Medical Officer 1 converted to point 1 be moved to point 2 with the retroactive difference paid. Circular No. 3 of 1 April 2025 then moved everyone along their band: three points for doctors in service before April 2022, two for those in service from April 2023, one for those from April 2024 (each point is 2.5% above the one before). Use the point on your latest payslip. None of this changes the duty rates, which are priced at the midpoint for every Medical Officer.

Step 2 · Rate card

Your rates per hour and per call

Multipliers from Award section 2(d). Every category, including Casualty and the other 50% categories, has its own rate for each of the three day types: a normal weekday, your first day off and your second day off (the two rest days of the seven-day week, which can fall on any day; public holidays are priced at the second-day-off rate, as the Ministry of Finance's comparison tables and the February 2026 holiday-rate settlement treat them). For April to October 2023, before the seven-day week, read "first day off" as Saturday and "second day off" as Sunday or a public holiday.

The boxes below describe one call and one session; they feed the per-call figures in the table and turn the shifts you count in Step 3 into hours. Since the seven-day week, a 24-hour call on a day off (8 a.m. to 8 a.m.) is made up of 16 hours of rostered duty, or of covering multiple locations, from 4 p.m. to 8 a.m. plus an 8-hour Casualty session from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., and the Casualty session is paid at the Casualty session rate for that day type, 50% above the Rostered Duty Rate (section 2(d)(iv)), whether or not you are covering several hospitals, because covering multiple locations is a separate rate under section 2(d)(ii). If your institution pays all 24 hours as rostered duty, put 24 in the first box and 0 in the second.

The seven-day week began on 1 November 2023 at the Ministry's hospitals and on 1 December 2024 at the University Hospital of the West Indies. Before the month chosen here the whole call is priced at the rostered or multiple-location rate for that day, so for April to October 2023 the two numbers are simply added together.

Step 3 · Your month

Duty in a typical month

Enter what you do in a month under each category, split by day type. Each row can be counted in shifts or sessions (a call is the hours set in Step 2, with its Casualty session added automatically for day-off calls from the month set there; a session is 4 hours unless you changed it) or entered straight in hours, whichever matches your claim forms; the hours the row adds up to are shown under it. Every row has its own first-day-off and second-day-off cells, including Casualty sessions. The example loaded is roughly a one-in-four on-call roster; the "seven-day week month" example is the month the Association itself costed in November 2024 (five 16-hour weekday calls, two calls on each day off, every day-off call made up of a 16-hour rostered stretch plus an 8-hour Casualty session), which at the old rates comes to J$406,239.24 for a Medical Officer 1, the figure in the Association's own workbook.

Step 4 · Incentive Allowance

Non-rostered doctors

Reinstated from 1 April 2023 by a two-to-one majority (Award section 3; dissent at paragraph 86). Eligibility follows the 2017-2021 Heads of Agreement categories.

Step 5 · Back-pay

Retroactive to 1 April 2023

Both the Emergency Duty Allowance formulas and the reinstated Incentive Allowance take effect from 1 April 2023 (Award section 2(e) and 3(a); paragraph 83). Each month is priced on that fiscal year's published scale with your Step 3 pattern, or with the earlier pattern you add below for the months before it changed. What was already paid is estimated from the old 2017 to 2021 rates (shown and editable further down) unless you enter your own monthly average.

Did your pattern of duty change during the period?

Your Step 3 pattern applies to every month unless you say otherwise here. Add a pattern for the months before a change and set the last month it applies to. The usual case: a pattern ending in October 2023 for April to October 2023, before the seven-day week began at most Ministry hospitals on 1 November 2023 (the February 2026 settlement with the Ministry fixes that date, or the date your own hospital started, as the point from which holiday rates and Casualty sessions on days off are corrected). The University Hospital only moved to the seven-day week on 1 December 2024, so its doctors would end the earlier pattern in November 2024. Each earlier pattern runs from the month after the previous one ends; your Step 3 pattern takes over after the last of them and runs to today.

The old 2017-2021 rates used for "already paid" (editable)

These are the Heads of Agreement rates the pay stations kept using after the contract expired, as scheduled in the Memorandum of Understanding of 16 March 2023 (Compensation Unit schedule of 1 December 2021, which the Memorandum says "continue to apply") and listed again by the Ministry of Finance under "current rates" in its comparison table of 29 August 2024: per hour for rostered and multiple-location duty, per four-hour session for ward and Casualty sessions, each by day type. They are the April 2021 figures (4% above 2020, which is why the Association's December 2021 arrears notice quoted a difference of J$590.73 per weekday Casualty session). They are shown for the grade chosen in Step 1 and for the grade set for each year below. If your pay station paid different figures, type yours over them; the back-pay changes at once.

Where the old rates came from, and three cases to watch

The old formula (Association briefing of February 2024, confirmed by the figures): base rate = the old scale's midpoint ÷ 2,080 hours; rostered duty = base × 1.25 on a weekday, × 1.30 on top for a Saturday or first day off, × 1.35 for a Sunday, holiday or second day off; covering multiple hospitals = the rostered rate × 1.25; a ward session = the end point of the old Medical Officer 2 scale ÷ 2,080 × 1.5 per hour, the same figure for every Medical Officer grade; a Casualty, Type C or cross-coverage session = the ward rate × 1.7. The Award keeps the shape (one base, day-type multipliers, sessional premiums) but moves the base to the restructured midpoint, lifts the weekday multiplier from 1.25 to 1.5 and sets the days off at 1.75 and 2.0, which is what the Association's June 2024 vote fixed as its minimum for acceptance.

Cross coverage was listed as a separate old rate per hour (J$3,709.91 weekday, J$4,822.90 first day off, J$5,008.40 second day off for Medical Officer 1 and Interns). The Casualty session row stands in for it here (J$3,839.77 an hour on a weekday), about 3.5% higher, so the back-pay for cross-coverage hours is understated by that much; type the per-hour figures × 4 over the Casualty row if you want it exact.

Type C hospitals. Many Type C doctors were paid at the ward sessional rate instead of the Casualty rate. The February 2026 settlement with the Ministry of Health corrects that separately, with retroactivity from 2009 for the Southern Region hospitals and disbursement promised for September 2026. Keep the Casualty rate here so that correction is not counted twice; the Award's back-pay is the difference between the Award's rate and the correct old rate.

University Hospital. The hospital has treated duties up to one in three as part of residents' ordinary work and paid extra hours only beyond that, and since 1 March 2026 it pays no sessional claims for residents rotating elsewhere. If that is you, the old-rate estimate overstates what you actually received; choose "I will enter my average per month" above and use your payslips.

Salary, duty and allowances, after deductions

A month's take-home when the Award's duty pay is added to your basic salary at your own point. Summary

Summary

The Award explained

What was decided, in plain language

On 5 October 2026 a three-member division of the Industrial Disputes Tribunal settled the dispute the Minister of Labour and Social Security referred to it on 15 January 2025: how public-sector doctors from Locum/Medical Intern to Medical Officer 3 are to be paid for the emergency duty they are contractually required to work beyond the forty-hour week. The hearing ran to fifty-three sittings between April 2025 and April 2026, with ten witnesses and one hundred and sixty-two exhibits. The Association was represented by Senator Lambert Brown and its executive; the Ministry by attorneys instructed through the Ministry of Finance.

The three things the Tribunal decided

  1. The doctor-specific framework stays. The Government wanted doctors moved onto the public-service-wide Extra Hours Worked policy (Circular No. 19): time-and-a-half on weekdays and the first rest day, double time on the second rest day and public holidays, no sessional premiums, a fifteen-hour weekly cap and written pre-approval. The Tribunal found that policy "operationally incompatible with continuous 24-hour public healthcare delivery and inimical to patient safety" and awarded the Association's own Cost-Reduction Proposal of 15 March 2024 instead. This part was unanimous.
  2. The Incentive Allowance is reinstated. The Ministry of Finance said the allowance had been absorbed into the restructured basic salaries in April 2023. The Tribunal found it was never lawfully removed: the Association protested in writing from the day the Memorandum of Understanding was signed, the savings clause (clause 3.11) preserved it and the conversion gave eligible doctors "no financial recognition" for it, which the Tribunal called an "uncompensated erasure". One member, Mrs. Bernita Locke, dissented on the ground that the allowance was outside the Terms of Reference.
  3. Everything runs from 1 April 2023. The Government asked for 1 August 2024. The Tribunal found no legal, contractual or equitable basis for that date: the allowance talks were supposed to finish by 31 March 2023, the delays were the State's and "medical officers cannot be financially penalised for administrative delays caused by the State".

Who must pay

The Ministry of Health and Wellness was the only employer named in the referral, but the Tribunal held that it acts as agent for the whole Government, so the Award binds the Ministry of Finance and the Public Service as central paymaster, all four Regional Health Authorities, the University Hospital of the West Indies, Bellevue Hospital, the Ministry of National Security for doctors in the Constabulary and Correctional Services and every direct employing authority of doctors in the bargaining unit (section 1(a)). Consultants are not in this bargaining unit; their association has its own matter before the Tribunal.

How the money is worked out

  • Hourly base: the midpoint of your grade's restructured scale (points 4 and 5 averaged) divided by 2,080 hours; for Interns, Locum Interns and Senior House Officers, the annual salary divided by 2,080. A Medical Officer 1 on point 1 is paid duty at the same hourly base as one on point 8.
  • Rostered emergency duty: 1.5 times the base on a normal weekday, 1.75 times on the first day off, 2.0 times on the second day off. All grades.
  • Covering multiple locations: 1.75, 2.0 and 2.25 times. Senior House Officers and Medical Officers 1 to 3.
  • Ward sessions: 25% above the rostered rate for that day. Casualty, Type C hospitals, Health Centres and cross-coverage: 50% above it.
  • Incentive Allowance: 40% of the scale midpoint per year for eligible non-rostered doctors in designated specialties and at the Ministry's Head Office; 55% for Medical Officers 2 and 3 serving as Medical Officer (Health); others as the 2017-2021 Heads of Agreement identifies them. A month with a rostered emergency duty shift earns no allowance.

The road here

1987 to 1990The Junior Doctors' Association sues the Ministry of Health. In 1990 Justice Clarke rules that neither wage legislation nor custom and practice gives doctors a right to premium overtime; the lesson is that the pay for compulsory emergency duty has to be negotiated and written down. The same case settles that the real employer is the Government of Jamaica.
1996The Seymour Award (Morin Seymour, Rex Nettleford, Lloyd Barnett) holds that the Emergency Duty Allowance "is different in nature from ordinary duty allowance and is paid in recognition of the inherent characteristics of the Junior Doctors' functions" and that the Incentive Allowance and the Emergency Duty Allowance are "one and the same allowance with a change of name".
2006 to 2021Successive Heads of Agreement carry two heads of pay: rostered duty at an hourly rate (125% of midpoint) with sessional categories and an Incentive Allowance of 40% or 55% of midpoint for doctors who cannot be rostered.
16 March 2023Under a Budget-week ultimatum, two of five negotiators sign the Memorandum of Understanding on the restructured basic scales. Allowance talks are deferred to 31 March 2023. Within twenty-five minutes of being told the Incentive Allowance is "no longer payable", the President emails her objection; at least eleven protest letters follow.
Oct 2023 to Nov 2024The seven-day, forty-hour week is imposed by circular; the Ministry of Finance issues the Extra Hours Worked policy; a second circular (36/2x) suspends it for doctors "pending further instructions" that are never issued. Doctors keep being paid at the expired 2017-2021 rates.
15 March 2024The Association tables its Cost-Reduction Proposal, collapsing five sessional categories into four. Its cost, J$30.75 billion a year, is about J$7.76 billion more than the Government's policy; the Government's first costing, J$140 billion, is later disowned by its own counsel as "flawed".
9 Dec 2024 to 15 Jan 2025Industrial action, a Resumption of Normality Agreement, conciliation at the Ministry of Labour and the referral to the Tribunal.
Apr 2025 to Apr 2026Fifty-three sittings. The Ministry's lead counsel concedes on the record that the flat policy "does not bind doctors at this point; it is a matter for the Tribunal".
Apr to Oct 2026Six months of silence after closing submissions; press statements from the Association, the consultants and the Confederation of Trade Unions; a twenty-four-hour withdrawal of service on 2 October. The Award is handed down on 5 October, forty-two months after the effective date it sets.

What the Award does not do

  • It does not set a payment date or a timetable. The formulas and the effective date are fixed; the pay stations still have to compute each doctor's hours month by month and the Ministry of Finance has to issue the implementing circular and find the funds.
  • It does not award interest on the back-pay and it does not itself change the seven-day roster rules; it prices the days off within them.
  • It is "final and conclusive" under section 12(4)(c) of the Labour Relations and Industrial Disputes Act. No court can set it aside except on a point of law. The Incentive Allowance limb, with its recorded dissent on jurisdiction, is the one part a challenge could realistically target.

Method and caveats

How this is calculated

  1. Hourly base. Medical Officers 1 to 3: scale midpoint (Point 4 + Point 5) ÷ 2, then ÷ 2,080 hours (40 hours × 52 weeks). Interns, Locum Interns and Senior House Officers: annual salary ÷ 2,080. Award section 2(b) and 2(c), paragraph 78(a).
  2. Rostered duty. Base × 1.5 (weekday), × 1.75 (first day off), × 2.0 (second day off; public holidays priced the same). Section 2(d)(i).
  3. Covering multiple locations. Base × 1.75 / 2.0 / 2.25. Senior House Officers and Medical Officers 1 to 3. Section 2(d)(ii).
  4. Ward sessions at 25% above the rostered rate for that day type; Casualty, Type C, Health Centre and cross-coverage at 50% above it, on weekdays and on both days off alike. Section 2(d)(iii) and (iv).
  5. Units. Every row in Step 3 is turned into hours before it is priced: calls × the hours in one call (Step 2), with each day-off call from the seven-day week onward split into its rostered or multiple-location hours and its Casualty session hours (priced at the Casualty session rate for that day type), sessions × the hours in one session, or the hours you typed. Hours × the rate for that category and day type gives each line of the working shown under the monthly result.
  6. Incentive Allowance. 40% or 55% of the scale midpoint per year, shown as the annual amount ÷ 12, removed for any month with rostered emergency duty hours (paragraph 85). For single-point grades the midpoint is the salary itself.
  7. Back-pay. Every month from April 2023 to the month you choose is priced on that fiscal year's published scale with the pattern in force for that month (your Step 3 pattern, or an earlier pattern you added in Step 5 for the months before it changed), then summed. "Already paid" at the old rates uses the 2017-2021 per-hour and per-four-hour-session rates the Ministry of Finance listed as "current" in its comparison table of 29 August 2024, as shown in the editable table in Step 5 (for Interns and Locum Interns the Senior House Officer session rates stand in where none was listed). The Incentive Allowance has not been paid since April 2023, so nothing is deducted for it.
  8. Net pay. Basic salary at your own point ÷ 12, plus the month's duty pay and allowance. Pension at the set percentage of basic salary; National Insurance on gross up to one twelfth of the annual ceiling; Education Tax on gross less National Insurance and pension; National Housing Trust on gross; income tax on the same statutory income above one twelfth of the annual threshold, with the part above one twelfth of J$6,000,000 at 30%. The back-pay rate the model suggests treats the lump sum as extra income in the year it is paid: no pension on it, National Insurance only where the ceiling has not already been reached and income tax at the marginal rate.

Read before quoting a number

  • These are estimates for discussion. Your pay station's recalculation from the actual rosters and claim forms is what will be paid and its payroll system applies the statutory rules to the cent.
  • One pattern is applied to every month it covers. If your grade changed, set the grade and the months counted for each year; if your pattern of duty changed, add the earlier pattern in Step 5 with the last month it applies to.
  • The old-rate estimate assumes you were paid correctly at the 2017-2021 rates. Many were not; the February 2026 settlement of Heads of Agreement breaches (holiday rate at the second-day-off rate from November 2023, Type C rates back to 2009, covering multiple hospitals back to 2006, compulsory weekend ward work from 2015, the MO1 point 2 placement from April 2023) is a separate stream of back-pay and is not counted here. If any of those corrections has not reached you, claim it on its own; do not reduce the old-rate figure here to make up for it, or the Award's back-pay will be overstated by the same amount.
  • The Award's own words tie the hourly base to "the 2022-2025 contract period" (section 2(b)). Pricing April 2026 onward on the new 2026-2028 scales assumes the same framework carries into the new contract, which is the Association's position; nothing in the Award says otherwise, but the figures for those months are projections until the Ministry of Finance confirms them.
  • "Ward Sessional Rate 25% above the Rostered Duty Rate" and "Premium Rates 50% above" are read here as 25% and 50% above the rostered rate for the day type the session falls on, the way the old sessional rates also carried Saturday and Sunday loadings and the way the Association's proposal was framed ("times the respective rostered duty rate"). A pay station reading them as 25% and 50% above the weekday rate alone would pay less for day-off sessions; the working panel shows each line so the difference can be isolated if that happens.
  • Allowances the calculator does not model (the meal allowance, J$80,000 a year under the Memorandum of Understanding; taxi; uniform; the one-off J$80,000 grant of 2025/26) sit outside these figures; add them to the "other" lines if you want them in your month.
